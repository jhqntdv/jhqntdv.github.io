## Introduction & Accounting Framework

Under **ASC 718 – Compensation—Stock Compensation**, share-based payment awards are accounted for using the **modified grant-date approach**.

The accounting treatment and valuation requirements depend fundamentally on the classification of the award and the nature of its vesting conditions:

* **Equity-Classified Awards:** Measured at fair value on the **grant date**. Once determined, this grant-date fair value is locked in and recognized as compensation expense over the requisite service period for awards that ultimately vest.
  * **Service & Performance Conditions:** Excluded from the grant-date fair value measurement. Instead, they govern the *number* of awards expected to vest and the accrual period. If a performance or service condition is not met, previously recognized compensation cost is reversed.
  * **Market Conditions:** Must be directly incorporated into the grant-date fair value (typically reducing the grant-date fair value per share). Crucially, compensation expense for awards with market conditions is recognized over the requisite service period regardless of whether the market hurdle is ultimately achieved.
* **Liability-Classified Awards:** Awards settling in cash (such as cash-settled Stock Appreciation Rights, SARs) or equity awards containing repurchase/put features are classified as liabilities. These awards must be **remeasured at fair value at each reporting date** until final settlement, with fair value changes flowing directly through earnings.

## When Valuation is Triggered: ASC 718 Valuation Events

In corporate practice, share-based compensation valuations are triggered by several distinct corporate and accounting events:

1. **Initial Grant Date Valuation:**
   * Issuance of options, restricted stock units (RSUs), or profits interests to employees or nonemployees in exchange for goods or services.
   * Issuance of equity-based awards to customers as sales incentives.
   * Issuance of equity instruments as consideration in asset acquisitions.
2. **Compensatory ESPPs:**
   * Employee Stock Purchase Plans that are compensatory (e.g., offering discounts greater than 5% or featuring look-back options) require option pricing models to value the embedded purchase rights.
3. **Reporting Date Remeasurements (Liability Awards):**
   * Periodic revaluation at each quarterly/annual reporting date for cash-settled SARs, phantom stock, or shares subject to mandatory company repurchase.
4. **Award Modifications (Type I through Type IV):**
   * Any change in the terms of an award (e.g., strike repricing, extension of contractual term, vesting acceleration). Valuation specialists must calculate the **incremental compensation cost**:
   $$
   \text{Incremental Cost} = \max\left(\text{Fair Value}_{\text{Post-Modification}} - \text{Fair Value}_{\text{Pre-Modification}}, \, 0\right)
   $$
5. **Classification Changes:**
   * Modifications causing an award to shift from equity to liability (requiring fair value measurement at the modification date) or liability to equity.
6. **Cash Settlements & Repurchases:**
   * Buying back vested equity awards for cash requires a fair value assessment at the repurchase date. Any cash paid in excess of the award's fair value represents additional compensation expense.
7. **Cancellations and Replacements:**
   * Canceling existing underwater awards and issuing new replacement awards is treated as a modification, requiring pre- and post-fair value determinations.
8. **Business Combinations (M&A Replacement Awards):**
   * Acquirers exchanging existing target employee awards must perform valuations to bifurcate the total replacement award fair value into:
     * **Purchase Consideration:** Attributable to pre-combination service (included in acquisition consideration).
     * **Post-Combination Compensation Cost:** Attributable to post-combination service (recognized in post-merger earnings).
9. **Equity Restructurings:**
   * Spin-offs, stock splits, or recapitalizations that alter award terms require anti-dilution fair value testing to ensure no unintended value transfer occurs.

## Valuation Models & Model Selection

ASC 718 does not mandate a single pricing model, but requires that the selected model reflects the economics of the contract and market participant assumptions.

> For an in-depth mathematical guide and Python implementations of numerical quadrature (composite trapezoidal rule) and the Likelihood Ratio Method (LRM) for structured equity compensation, see [Numerical Methods in Option Pricing](/posts/Numerical-Method-in-Option-Pricing/).

### 1. Closed-Form Models (Black-Scholes-Merton)
* **Application:** Standard European-style employee stock options with straightforward vesting and static exercise assumptions.
* **Limitations:** Assumes constant volatility, a constant risk-free rate, and a single static expected term. It cannot accommodate dynamic employee early exercise, blackout periods, or post-vesting employment termination.

### 2. Lattice Models (Binomial / Trinomial Trees)
* **Application:** Recommended by the FASB and major accounting firms (e.g., EY) as superior to Black-Scholes for complex employee stock options.
* **Core Advantage:** Lattice models evaluate backward induction across discrete time steps, explicitly incorporating:
  * **Suboptimal Early Exercise Behavior:** Incorporating an exercise multiple (e.g., employees historically exercise when the stock reaches $2.0 \times$ the strike).
  * **Post-Vesting Termination Rates (Forfeiture/Departure):** Modeling voluntary and involuntary employee turnover dynamically across the tree.
  * **Blackout Periods:** Restricting early exercise during corporate quiet periods.

### 3. Monte Carlo Simulation
* **Application:** Required whenever awards feature **market conditions** (e.g., Total Shareholder Return (TSR) benchmarks against an index, target stock price hurdles, or profits interest distribution waterfalls).
* **Dynamics:** Simulates thousands of Geometric Brownian Motion (GBM) paths under the risk-neutral measure:
  $$
  S_t = S_0 \exp\left[\left(r - q - \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2\right)t + \sigma \sqrt{t} \, Z\right], \quad Z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)
  $$
  Each path tracks contractual vesting conditions, computes the terminal payoff, and discounts back at the risk-free rate.

## Mandatory Inputs & Assumptions

Regardless of the model chosen, ASC 718 mandates six fundamental economic inputs:

| Input Parameter | Model Role | Practical Estimation & Auditor Considerations |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Current Stock Price ($S_0$)** | Baseline equity value | Observable closing price for public entities; for private firms, established via contemporaneous 409A / equity allocation valuations. |
| **Exercise Price ($K$)** | Contractual strike price | Defined by the award agreement; typically set equal to $S_0$ on the grant date for tax (IRC 409A) qualification. |
| **Expected Term ($T$)** | Option maturity horizon | For BSM, estimated via historical exercise data or the SEC Staff Accounting Bulletin (SAB) Topic 14 "simplified method". In lattice models, term is derived endogenously from early exercise and turnover assumptions. |
| **Expected Volatility ($\sigma$)** | Price dispersion measure | For public firms, blended historical and implied volatility; for private entities, computed from a curated peer group of comparable public companies over a period matching the expected term. |
| **Risk-Free Interest Rate ($r$)** | Discount rate under risk-neutral measure | Yield on U.S. Treasury zero-coupon bonds matching the expected term of the award. |
| **Expected Dividend Yield ($q$)** | Continuous cash drag | Historic and expected future dividend payout rate over the expected term. |

## Technical Valuation & Accounting Housekeeping Notes

In valuation practice and financial statement audits, several critical rules dictate how valuations must be structured:

* **No Upfront Cost Recognition:**  
  Entities are strictly prohibited from recognizing total compensation cost upfront at the grant date with an offsetting "contra-equity" or "contra-liability" account. Compensation cost must be accrued systematically over the requisite service period as services are rendered.
* **Graded Vesting Attribution Constraints:**  
  For awards with graded vesting schedules (e.g., 25% vesting per year over four years):
  * Pure service-condition awards allow entities to choose between the **straight-line method** or the **graded-vesting attribution method**.
  * Awards with **performance or market conditions** are strictly prohibited from using straight-line attribution; they must use the tranche-by-tranche graded attribution method (treating each vesting tranche as an independent award with its own expected term and valuation).
* **The "Derived Service Period" is Fixed:**  
  When an award contains a market condition, a Monte Carlo simulation establishes a "derived requisite service period" (the median time required to satisfy the market condition). Once established at the grant date, this service period is **fixed and cannot be adjusted**, even if management's subsequent expectations regarding when the market hurdle will be cleared change.
* **Nonrecourse Notes Are Deemed Options:**  
  If an employee purchases company shares using a nonrecourse promissory note (where the collateral is solely the underlying shares), the transaction is economically equivalent to a stock option with a strike equal to the note's face value plus accrued interest. It must be valued and accounted for under ASC 718 as an option grant.
* **Repurchase Features & Liability Classification:**  
  If an award contains a repurchase or clawback feature that permits the company to repurchase the shares at less than fair value within six months of issuance, the holder has not borne the risks and rewards of equity ownership for a mature holding period. The award must be classified as a **liability** and remeasured at fair value each period until settled.
